Glossary List

3D Parallel Training 3D parallel training is a distributed training strategy that combines data, model, and pipeline parallelism across three dimensions to optimize computational efficiency, reduce memory constraints, and enable scalable training of large language models.

Activation Function Activation function is a mathematical operation applied to the output of a neural network layer, introducing non-linear transformations to enable large language models to learn and represent learn complex patterns and relationships in data.

Agent In the context of large language model, an agent refers to an autonomous entity that utilizes the model’s capabilities to perform tasks, make decisions, or interact with users based on natural language input.

Artificial General Intelligence Artificial general intelligence (AGI) is a hypothetical form of machine intelligence capable of autonomously understanding, learning, and performing any intellectual task with human-level adaptability and reasoning.

Attention Mechanism Attention mechanism is a computational technique that dynamically assigns varying weights to different parts of the input data, enabling the model to focus on contextually relevant elements during processing, particularly through self-attention in Transformer architectures to capture dependencies between words or tokens.

Base Language Model A base language model refers to a language model that has only undergone pre-training, typically using next-token prediction tasks.

Causal Decoder Architecture Causal decoder architecture is a Transformer-based architecture used in autoregressive language models that generates text sequentially by restricting each token’s attention to preceding tokens through masked self-attention mechanisms, ensuring predictions depend only on past context.

Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning refers to a reasoning process that enhances complex problem-solving by explicitly breaking down tasks into sequential intermediate steps, mimicking human-like reasoning to improve accuracy and interpretability.

Data Curriculum In the context of large language model, a data curriculum refers to the structured and sequential organization of various datasets used to train the model, designed to optimize learning efficiency and performance.

Data Mix In the context of large language model, data mix refers to the proportion, distribution, and sources of various datasets combined during training to ensure balanced learning across tasks, domains, and modalities for optimal model performance.

Decoding In the context of large language models, decoding is the process of generating text output by selecting tokens from the model’s probability distribution over its vocabulary, guided by strategies such as greedy search, beam search, or sampling.

Emergent Ability In the context of large language model, emergent ability refers to capabilities that unpredictably arise when the model reaches a certain scale in parameters, training data, or computational resources, surpassing those observed in smaller models.

Encoder-Decoder Architecture Encoder-decoder architecture is a neural network framework where an encoder processes input sequences into contextualized representations, and a decoder generates corresponding output sequences in a step-by-step way.

Fine-Tuned Language Model Fine-tuned language model, a.k.a., instruct language model, refers to a language model that has undergone additional training on specific tasks or datasets, often using supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, to align its outputs with desired instructions or user intents.

Greedy Search Greedy search is a decoding strategy in large language models where the token with the highest probability is selected at each generation step independently of future sequences.

Hallucination In the context of large language models, hallucination refers to the generation of text that appears plausible but is fabricated or inaccurate, not grounded in the input data, training corpus, or factual reality.

Human Alignment Human alignment refers to the process of optimizing model behavior to adhere to human values, ethical standards, and user intentions, ensuring outputs are helpful, honest, and harmless through methods.

In-Context Learning In-context learning (ICL) is the capability of a large language model to solve tasks by conditioning on input-output examples or task instructions provided in the input prompt without any updates to the model’s parameters.

Inference Inference, in the context of large language models, refers to the process of generating predictions or outputs based on input data by utilizing the model’s learned knowledge and capabilities.

Instruction Tuning Instruction tuning, a.k.a., supervised fine-tuning, is a process in which a large language model is fine-tuned on a curated dataset of input-output pairs, where the inputs are natural language instructions and the outputs are their corresponding responses.

Language Model A language model is a probabilistic generative model that learns patterns and structures from extensive text data to understand, generate, and predict human language.

Large Language Model A large language model is an advanced neural network based on transformer architecture, trained on massive text datasets through deep learning techniques to comprehend, generate, and manipulate human language effectively across diverse applications.

Large Reasoning Model A large reasoning model is a specialized variant of a large language model that is enhanced with advanced architectures or training methodologies, which is designed to perform complex tasks by executing extended reasoning processes.

Long CoT Reasoning Long CoT reasoning is a technique in large language models where complex problems are addressed through an extended thought process of explicit, intermediate reasoning steps, allowing for deeper logical analysis and improved accuracy.

Long Context Model A long context model is a type of large language model specifically designed to process and generate coherent responses across extended text sequences by effectively managing and leveraging larger context windows, ensuring a comprehensive understanding of long texts.

Mixed-Precision Training Mixed-precision training is a computational technique that combines both 16-bit and 32-bit floating-point precision during training to accelerate computation, reduce memory usage, and maintain numerical stability while preserving model accuracy in large language models.

Mixture-of-Experts Model Mixture-of-experts (MoE) model is a neural network architecture where a gating mechanism dynamically routes each input to a subset of specialized expert sub-networks, enabling more efficient computation while maintaining high performance.

Model Distillation Model distillation is a technique where a smaller, more efficient model (the “student”) is trained to replicate the knowledge and performance of a larger, computationally intensive model (the “teacher”) by mimicking its outputs, representations, or decision boundaries.

Model Pruning Model pruning is the process of reducing the model’s size and computational complexity by removing less important parameters, such as weights or neurons, while maintaining comparable performance.

Model Quantization Model quantization is a technique used to reduce the computational and memory requirements of model copies by approximating their weights and activations with lower-precision numerical formats, such as 8-bit integers, while preserving model performance as much as possible.

Multi-Agent Systems Multi-agent systems are computational frameworks where multiple autonomous models or agents collaborate, compete, or communicate to solve complex tasks, simulate human-like interactions, or enhance decision-making through distributed reasoning and role specialization.

Multi-Head Attention Multi-head attention is a mechanism in Transformer models that employs multiple parallel attention heads to simultaneously process and integrate diverse contextual relationships across input tokens.

Normalization In the context of neural networks, normalization refers to the process of scaling, adjusting, or transforming input data, model weights, or output probabilities to ensure stability, consistency, or improved performance during training or inference.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Parameter-efficient fine-tuning is a technique in large language models that updates only a small subset of model parameters or introduces additional lightweight parameters during training, enabling task-specific adaptation with reduced computational and storage costs compared to full model fine-tuning.

Planning Planning, in the context of large language models, refers to the strategic process of determining the sequence and structure of generated content to achieve coherent, contextually relevant, and goal-oriented outputs.

Position Encodings Position encodings refer to mechanisms that inject sequential position information into input tokens, enabling Transformers to capture the order of elements in a sequence despite processing them in parallel.

Post-Training Post-training refers to the phase in large language model development where a pre-trained model undergoes additional adaptation, optimization, or alignment processes—such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning from human feedback—to enhance its performance, efficiency, or suitability for specific tasks or ethical guidelines.

Pre-Trained Language Model A pre-trained language model is a neural network model initially trained on a large corpus of unlabeled text data to understand and generate human language, serving as a foundational component for developing more specialized models.

Pre-Training Pre-training refers to the process of initially training a large language model on a vast corpus of text data to learn general language patterns and representations before fine-tuning it on specific tasks or datasets.

Prompt Prompt is a textual input or instruction provided to a language model to guide its response by specifying the desired content, style, context, or format of the generated output.

Random Sampling Random sampling is a decoding strategy in large language models where the next token is probabilistically selected from the model’s predicted distribution at each generation step.

Reinforcement Learning In the context of large language models, reinforcement learning refers to a machine learning paradigm in which language models improve their performance and decision-making by receiving feedback in the form of rewards or penal-ties, enabling the model to optimize its behavior or output over successive iterations.

Rejection Sampling Rejection sampling is a technique used in language models to refine output generation by sampling candidate outputs from the model’s probabilistic distribution and discarding those that do not meet a predefined criterion, ensuring higher-quality or more relevant responses.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a technique in large language models that combines information retrieval with text generation, allowing the model to access and incorporate external knowledge sources to enhance the accuracy and relevance of its responses.

Scaling Law In the context of language models, scaling laws are empirical principles that describe a predictable power-law relationship between improvements in model performance and increases in model size, training dataset size, and computational resources allocated during training.

Tokenization Tokenization is the process of segmenting input text into smaller semantic units, such as words, subwords, or symbols.

Top-k Sampling Top-k sampling is a decoding strategy used in language models where, at each step, the model selects the next word from the top k most probable candidates, allowing for more diverse and controlled text generation by limiting the sampling to a subset of the vocabulary.
