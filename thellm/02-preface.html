Preface

The release of ChatGPT in late November 2022 had a significant impact on the field of artificial intelligence (AI), quickly propelling large language models (LLMs) into the spotlight of both societal and academic discourse. This breakthrough represents a substantial advancement in AI technology and has sparked widespread interest in the foundational technologies that enable these models to perform so remarkably. Understanding these underlying technologies has become a central focus for researchers and engineers, driving a wave of exploration and innovation within the field. It’s important to recognize that language model technology did not emerge overnight. Its development has progressed through several stages: statistical language models, neural language models, pre-trained language models, and now large language models. Each stage reflects the dedicated efforts and achievements of numerous researchers.

Given OpenAI’s pivotal role in advancing language model technology, we begin by reviewing its research journey in this field to gain a holistic understanding of its technical evolution. In 2015, OpenAI was officially founded with the goal of advancing general artificial intelligence. The early OpenAI team launched research initiatives across several key areas, including reinforcement learning, multimodal models, and language models. Among these, the team led by Ilya Sutskever focused primarily on language models. When Google introduced the Transformer model, based on the attention mechanism, in 2017, the OpenAI team quickly recognized its potential, believing it could provide an ideal architecture for large-scale, scalable training. Building on this insight, the team developed the GPT series models, starting with GPT-1 in 2018. GPT-1 was capable of solving downstream tasks using a “pre-training + fine-tuning” approach. The subsequent GPT-2 and GPT-3 models significantly improved performance by expanding both the pre-training data and model parameter scale, establishing a robust, general-purpose framework for solving tasks based on natural language. Building upon GPT-3, OpenAI further advanced model performance with techniques such as code training, human alignment, and tool utilization, ultimately releasing the GPT-3.5 API. In November 2022, ChatGPT was

launched, enabling users to interact with the model in a conversational manner and perform a wide range of tasks, showcasing the capabilities of the language model. In March 2023, OpenAI further introduced the GPT-4 model, significantly enhancing the model’s capabilities and marking a new milestone. This was followed by the release of the multimodal-capable GPT-4V model, as well as the GPT-4o, further expanding the model’s potential and capacities. More recently, OpenAI’s efforts in test-time scaling of o-series models (e.g., o1 and o3) have also achieved great success.

Looking back at the development history of LLMs, two key aspects stand out. The first aspect is the exploration of the scaling limits through a highly scalable learning paradigm. Building on the Transformer architecture, language models can be extended to hundreds of billions or even trillions of parameters when trained with the general, unified pre-training task of predicting the next token. The second aspect is the emphasis on a data-centric training approach. Unlike the pre-training models of the BERT era, the success of LLMs is more closely tied to the data itself and its strategic use, with high-quality, large-scale datasets serving as the foundational pillars of these models. While these ideas may seem straightforward, identifying and decisively pursuing this path amidst numerous early technical approaches has been a key factor in OpenAI’s success. However, since the release of GPT-3, the OpenAI team has remained relatively tight-lipped about the technical details of their models, with most public reports focusing primarily on evaluation results.

To understand and master language model techniques, the “know-how” gained from firsthand experience is of great value to both researchers and engineers. Only by engaging directly with the core technology can we truly understand which problems are most critical and develop effective solutions. However, training large, high-performance models requires significant computational resources. Currently, the most advanced LLMs are primarily developed in industry, and this trend is likely to intensify over time. A major challenge facing the academic community today is the scarcity of these resources for training and experimenting with LLMs. This lack of access to computational power creates a gap in firsthand experience with LLMs, particularly in training them from scratch. Pre-training language models typically involves numerous engineering techniques and intricate details that are often not fully discussed in existing scientific papers. The current limitations in computational resources prevent researchers from conducting in-depth experimentation and gaining valuable insights into model pre-training.

Fortunately, both academia and industry are increasingly recognizing the importance of openness. We are witnessing a growing number of open foundation models, code repositories, and technical reports, all of which play a crucial role in enhancing the transparency of language model technology. Through openness and collaboration, we can leverage the collective wisdom of humanity to accelerate the development of AI research. With these disclosed technologies, researchers are now able to successfully train powerful language models that achieve impressive results, provided they have access to adequate computational resources. As more core technologies are shared and made available, the transparency of language model technology will continue to improve.

To better organize and disseminate the latest advancements in LLMs, we posted a review paper titled “A Survey of Large Language Models” on arXiv in March 2023, which we continue to update and improve to reflect recent progress in the field. The survey paper has now reached its 15th version, comprising 140 pages of main text and over 1064 references. Since its release, we have received extensive feedback from readers, which has greatly contributed to the refinement of the survey. To further assist readers in learning about language model technology, we decided to write a Chinese textbook based on this survey, which was completed in November 2024. This Chinese textbook on LLMs is aimed at senior undergraduates with a background in deep learning, as well as junior graduate students, serving as an introductory technical resource. In preparing the textbook, we conducted a thorough review of classic papers, relevant code repositories, and existing textbooks, from which we extracted core concepts, mainstream algorithms, and models. These topics were then systematically organized and presented. Each chapter’s initial draft was revised multiple times to ensure clarity and accuracy. After completing the Chinese version of the textbook, we were invited by Springer to translate it into English, which ultimately resulted in the current English edition.

During the writing process, we became acutely aware of the limitations of our own knowledge and abilities. Despite our best efforts, some omissions or inaccuracies were inevitable. We warmly invite readers to provide feedback and suggestions, and those who offer insightful comments will be acknowledged on our website. We consider the process of writing this book a valuable learning journey and look forward to meaningful

exchanges with readers, gaining insights from research peers along the way. This book received invaluable help and support from the AI Box group at Renmin University of China. The group members (listed in alphabetical order by last name) include Yushuo Chen, Zhipeng Chen, Xiaoxue Cheng, Zican Dong, Yifan Du, Yupeng Hou, Jinhao Jiang, Yifan Li, Peiyu Liu, Zikang Liu, Yingqian Min, Ruiyang Ren, Wenqi Sun, Xinyu Tang, Zhen Tian, Xiaolei Wang, Yuhao Wang, Lanling Xu, Chen Yang, Jinxia Yang, Beichen Zhang, Gaowei Zhang, Junjie Zhang, and Bowen Zheng. We express our heartfelt gratitude for the hard work of these students. Without their continuous support and assistance, the completion of this book would not have been possible.

In summary, the field of language model technology is evolving at an unprecedented pace, with numerous fundamental principles and key technologies yet to be fully explored and refined. For researchers, the study of LLMs presents a wealth of innovative opportunities and continues to be a deeply engaging area of exploration. As technological advancements progress and knowledge is openly shared, we are confident that future breakthroughs in AI will catalyze even greater achievements, profoundly transforming and benefiting an ever-expanding range of disciplines.
